# Source Hierarchy

Use the highest suitable level that directly supports the claim. Search ranking is not authority.

## Level 1: primary or authoritative

Legislation, government departments, regulators, official institutional pages, official product documentation, standards bodies, original papers, official datasets, repositories, release notes, and direct policies. These are the default basis for `VERIFIED` when they directly establish the claim and are sufficiently current.

## Level 2: high-quality secondary

Systematic reviews, expert explanation, reputable professional analysis, and high-quality investigative reporting. Use for context, corroboration, or interpretation. Explain any case where it outweighs a Level 1 source.

## Level 3: discovery

Search results, aggregators, job boards, course directories, third-party databases, and community indexes. Use them to locate primary pages. Do not cite a search-results page as final evidence.

## Level 4: leads only

Forums, social posts, Reddit, unsourced blogs, AI-generated pages, copied adverts, scraped listings, anonymous claims, and unverified screenshots. They may identify a lead but normally cannot establish a material claim alone.

When sources conflict, authority is one factor alongside date, jurisdiction, scope, version, terminology, and directness. Never choose a source merely because it supports the preferred conclusion.
