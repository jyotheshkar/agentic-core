# MOA Intent Psychology

Distinguish the stated request from the goal it appears intended to serve. Consider only signals that materially affect framing: cognitive load, information overload, decision fatigue, procrastination, avoidance, sunk cost, urgency, fear, confirmation bias, overconfidence, indecision, motivation, action bias, social pressure, preference conflict, or short-term impulse.

Use tentative language and evidence from the request. Do not diagnose, mind-read, moralize, or manipulate. Do not turn every task into a psychology exercise.

Return: stated request, likely objective, relevant signal with evidence, routing implication, and uncertainty. If no behavioral signal matters, say `none`.
