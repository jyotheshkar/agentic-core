# Game Theory and Incentives

Use ordinary incentive analysis unless formal game structure materially improves the decision.

## Workflow

1. Define the focal outcome and the actors able to influence it.
2. For each actor, map objectives, constraints, incentives, authority, alternatives, information, and cost of delay or disagreement.
3. Separate stated positions from underlying interests. Identify information asymmetry and principal-agent conflicts.
4. Assess bargaining position using alternatives, replaceability, timing, credible evidence, and control over scarce resources. Do not confuse confidence with leverage.
5. Map likely moves, responses, and counter-moves. Include cooperation, competition, non-response, escalation, and face-saving routes where plausible.
6. Evaluate signals and commitments for cost, credibility, reversibility, and how other actors may interpret them.
7. Recommend a strategy that aligns incentives, improves information, protects the downside, and preserves a credible alternative.

Return the actor map, incentive conflicts, power and information assessment, likely response range, strategic options, and recommended move. State assumptions about actors explicitly.

Read [strategic interaction method](references/strategic-interaction-method.md) for negotiation, repeated interaction, multi-party conflict, or principal-agent problems.

Do not presume irrationality, manipulate vulnerabilities, invent private motives, threaten without a credible and acceptable follow-through, or force equilibrium language onto a simple conversation.
