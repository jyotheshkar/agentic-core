# Strategic Interaction Method

## Actor table

For each actor record: desired outcome, minimum acceptable outcome, authority, constraints, alternatives, private information that may matter, observable signals, and likely response range. Mark inferences as uncertain.

## Bargaining

Estimate each side's best alternative if no agreement is reached, cost of delay, switching cost, and deadline. Leverage comes from credible alternatives and asymmetric costs, not from aggressive language.

## Signals and commitments

A signal is more credible when it is costly to fake or independently verifiable. A commitment is credible when the actor can and will carry it out. Avoid commitments that destroy valuable flexibility without improving the other side's incentives.

## Repeated interaction

Account for reputation, reciprocity, retaliation, learning, and future cooperation. A short-term gain can be strategically inferior if it damages a valuable repeated relationship.

## Safer negotiation sequence

Gather information, establish value, ask with a defensible rationale, provide options, allow a face-saving response, protect the fallback, and set a calm decision deadline. Identify the walk-away condition before negotiating.
