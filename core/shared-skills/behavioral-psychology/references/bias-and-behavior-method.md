# Bias and Behavior Method

## Evidence ladder

Use this order from strongest to weakest:

1. repeated observed behavior under similar conditions;
2. a specific action or commitment with consequences;
3. stated intention supported by incentives and ability;
4. stated intention alone;
5. interpretation based only on a general bias label.

Do not promote a lower rung into a confident prediction.

## Competing explanations

For each claimed mechanism, test at least one alternative. Apparent procrastination may be unclear scope, overload, low expected value, missing capability, or avoidance. Apparent status quo bias may instead reflect high switching cost. Apparent loss aversion may reflect a genuinely asymmetric downside.

## Multi-actor analysis

For each actor record goals, incentives, constraints, information, likely action range, and evidence. Distinguish what the actor says, what the actor has done, and what the environment rewards. Avoid mind-reading.

## Intervention design

Prefer transparent interventions that improve judgment or execution without coercion: independent criteria written before new evidence, reversible trials, smaller commitments, implementation intentions, reduced choice count, default calendar blocks, cooling-off periods, accountability chosen by the person, and explicit stop rules.

Report uncertainty when behavior depends on context that is not observed.
