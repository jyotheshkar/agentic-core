# Behavioral Psychology

Treat psychology as a set of testable decision hypotheses, not labels for people.

## Workflow

1. Identify the decision, actors, stakes, time pressure, and behavior that matters.
2. Separate observed behavior and stated intent from interpretations about motives.
3. Test only plausible mechanisms: confirmation bias, sunk cost, loss aversion, status quo bias, optimism or planning fallacy, anchoring, recency, social proof, authority, scarcity, emotional reasoning, ego or identity protection, avoidance, procrastination, overconfidence, indecision, cognitive load, decision fatigue, and incentive-driven behavior.
4. For each material mechanism, cite the prompt evidence, name at least one competing explanation, and rate confidence qualitatively.
5. Compare stated intention with incentives, friction, capability, and likely follow-through. For other actors, model a range of plausible responses rather than asserting a hidden motive.
6. Recommend a debiasing or behavior-support intervention that preserves autonomy, such as a cooling-off period, precommitted criterion, smaller reversible step, changed default, reduced friction, independent check, or decision deadline.
7. State what observation would confirm or weaken the analysis.

Return decision-relevant cues, plausible mechanisms, alternative explanations, likely behavioral effects, confidence, and safeguards. Mention only mechanisms that could change the decision.

Read [bias and behavior method](references/bias-and-behavior-method.md) when several actors, identity effects, or multiple competing biases are involved.

Do not diagnose psychiatric conditions, manipulate vulnerabilities, infer certainty about motives, excuse harmful behavior, or treat a bias label as proof. If evidence is thin, say that psychology is not decision-determinative.
