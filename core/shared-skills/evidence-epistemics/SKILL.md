# Evidence Epistemics

Calibrate conclusions to the available evidence. Do not create false precision.

## Workflow

1. Extract the claims that materially affect the conclusion.
2. Identify source, date, provenance, directness, independence, and incentives.
3. Classify each claim as FACT, STRONG EVIDENCE, WEAK EVIDENCE, INFERENCE, ESTIMATE, ASSUMPTION, SPECULATION, or UNKNOWN.
4. Prefer current primary evidence for factual and time-sensitive claims. Treat user-provided facts as user-provided unless independently verified.
5. Reconcile contradictions by comparing definitions, methods, dates, populations, incentives, and source independence.
6. Distinguish correlation, mechanism, temporal order, confounding, and causation.
7. State uncertainty and confidence qualitatively unless a defensible quantitative basis exists.
8. Name missing evidence and what would change the conclusion.

Return a concise claim ledger and calibrated conclusion. Search current authoritative sources when the task requires live verification and tools permit it.

Read [claim classifications](references/claim-classifications.md) for label thresholds. Read [source evaluation](references/source-evaluation.md) when sources conflict, may be stale, or make causal claims.

Do not upgrade repetition into corroboration, popularity into authority, correlation into causation, or an absence of evidence into evidence of absence without justification.
