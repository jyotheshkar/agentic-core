# Claim Classifications

- `FACT`: directly established for the stated scope by reliable evidence, with relevant definitions and freshness checked.
- `STRONG EVIDENCE`: multiple strong independent sources or one highly direct authoritative source supports the claim, with limited plausible alternatives.
- `WEAK EVIDENCE`: some relevant support exists, but method, directness, independence, sample, or freshness limits confidence.
- `INFERENCE`: a reasoned conclusion from established premises, not directly observed.
- `ESTIMATE`: an approximate value derived from inputs, assumptions, or a model.
- `ASSUMPTION`: used as true without sufficient support for the current purpose.
- `SPECULATION`: a possible explanation or outcome with little supporting evidence.
- `UNKNOWN`: decision-relevant information is not established.

Labels apply to a precise claim, time, population, and definition. A claim can move between labels as evidence changes. Always identify the evidence that would most efficiently change the label or conclusion.
