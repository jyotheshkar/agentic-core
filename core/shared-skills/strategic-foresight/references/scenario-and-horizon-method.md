# Scenario and Horizon Method

## Horizon discipline

Choose dates or intervals appropriate to the decision. Record what can change before each horizon. Separate consequences caused by the decision from background trends.

## Scenario construction

Build scenarios around one or two critical uncertainties, not around desired emotional tones. Each scenario needs a causal chain, affected actors, decision implications, and observable signposts.

- BASE: current trajectory with ordinary variation.
- BEST: favorable but plausible conditions, not a fantasy ceiling.
- WORST: material downside with plausible causes and response options.
- SURPRISE: a discontinuity or actor response omitted by the conventional frame.

## Robustness test

Classify actions as robust across scenarios, scenario-dependent, hedging, information-gathering, or irreversible. Prefer actions that preserve valuable options unless delay has a greater cost.

## Signposts

For each critical uncertainty, name a measurable early indicator, observation cadence, and decision trigger. A scenario exercise without signposts should not create confidence.

## Path dependency

Identify commitments that create technical, financial, legal, social, identity, or career lock-in. Estimate exit cost and the latest safe point to change course.
